Face Retirement was a Flash site for Merrill Edge, Bank of America’s online brokerage, launched in December 2012 by Starcom MediaVest Group. It started from a Stanford study: people find it hard to save for a future self who feels like a stranger, and those shown an aged image of themselves were more willing to put money away.

Visitors entered their age and gender, then took a photo with their webcam. A face aging algorithm turned the photo into a row of future selves, a decade apart, all the way to 107. Each age came with the year it would arrive and what things might cost by then, so a face at 107 sat beside a $39.88 gallon of gas in 2082. From there, visitors could share their older selves on Facebook or look at opening a Merrill Edge IRA.

I worked at Publicis, Starcom’s sister company in the same building, as a shared resource on the project. As Frontend Technical Lead, I built the front end of the site in Flash.

The most involved part was the webcam capture: asking for the camera, helping visitors frame their face, and taking a photo the aging algorithm could work with. It needed no custom image processing, only Flash’s own camera API, but it was the step every visitor had to get through before they saw anything.

It was picked up widely, from WIRED, Ad Age, and Fast Company to CNBC, Allure, and NBC’s local stations. More than 450,000 people tried it in its first months, and around one in ten shared their aged photo on Facebook.

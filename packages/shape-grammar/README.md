Shape Grammars

A small shape grammar engine and an endless page of the two-rule hexagon grammar: its rules at the top, then rows of four finished runs that load as you scroll, each row a level more complex than the last. The first row is a single step of the plain rules; each level adds a step and room for more hexagons, until about the ninth row, where runs reach eight steps and 500 hexagons. About a third of runs are asymmetric. Regenerate (or R) starts again from the top. The other grammars are still there with `?rule=triangle` or `?rule=diamond`.

The rules, simplest first, are the offset triangle, where a triangle grows a copy of itself turned upside down, sharing the line of one edge and slid half a side along it (a copy that would land exactly on an existing triangle is skipped); and the square and diamond, where a square grows a diamond on one side, a square half its area turned 45° and centred on that side. Either way the new shape is one the rule applies to in turn. The third grammar has two rules on hexagons: one puts a half-size hexagon centred on the midpoint of an edge, the other puts a full-size neighbour against an edge. Rule 1 also runs backwards, making a hexagon the half-size one on the edge of a hexagon twice its size. Each edge of a hexagon takes one rule, once. Each step gives the six edges a pattern of rules that repeats every 1, 2, 3 or 6 edges, applied to every hexagon in its own frame; a first step that repeats every 1 or 2 edges makes the whole design six- or three-fold symmetric, and one that repeats every 3 or 6 makes it asymmetric. Runs of more than one step use both rules, and runs stay legible: at most three sizes (the start, half it and twice it) besides the chains, an inset starts a chain, the inset rule applied again and again along the same line, halving each time toward the centre of the hexagon next door (drawn out in full at once, and taking no other rule), hexagons of one size never overlap, and a spot two moves in one step both want goes to neither, so the symmetry holds. Each run fills its own cell, and a row never shows the same design twice.

Most runs pick one rule and one set of edges each step and apply them to every shape in its own frame, so the design keeps the symmetry of its first step; the rest add one shape at a time on a random free edge. Every run is drawn at the same scale.

- `src/geometry.js`: affine transforms (`translate`, `rotate`, `scale`, `compose`) and geometry (`rect`, `square`, `triangle`, `polygon`, `polyline`).
- `src/grammar.js`: the engine. A `shape` is geometry placed by a transform, with an optional label. A `rule` names a label and gives the shapes that replace it, written in the matched shape's own frame, so it applies wherever that label appears at any position, rotation, scale or reflection. Rules can be functions for random variation, take `when` guards and `weight`s, and can keep `data` on a shape (the diamond rule keeps each square's free sides). `derive` runs a `grammar` from its axiom, either in parallel (every labelled shape each step, like an L-system) or sequentially (one shape and rule picked at random each step), and returns every state.
- `src/grammars.js`: the offset triangle, square and diamond, and two-rule hexagon grammars, symmetric and one at a time.
- `src/render.js` and `src/main.js`: SVG drawing and the page.

Matching is on labels, not geometry, so the engine doesn't find emergent subshapes where lines happen to overlap.

- `npm run dev --workspace=shape-grammar` serves it with hot reload at http://127.0.0.1:5194.
- `npm run build --workspace=shape-grammar` writes `dist/`, which the site build copies to `/sketches/shape-grammar/`.
